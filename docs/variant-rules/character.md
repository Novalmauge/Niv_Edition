# Character Creation

Before you roll your first die or write your backstory, review these guidelines to ensure your character fits the mechanical and narrative scope of the campaign.

## Available Sources

To maintain thematic consistency—whether we are exploring the Victorian high fantasy of Halione or surviving the gothic horror of Wightlaw—character options are currently limited to the following.

* **Core Rulebooks:** *Player's Handbook*, *Xanathar's Guide to Everything*, *Tasha's Cauldron of Everything*.
* **Homebrew Compendium:** Any custom species, classes, or origins explicitly listed in this repository.
* **Banned Content:** Currently there is nothing banned, and I don't tend to ban anything, but everything is subject to balancing.

!!! info "DM's Thoughts: Curation Over Restriction"
    Restricting sources isn't about limiting your fun; it is about preserving the tone of the setting. By curating the list, we ensure every character feels mechanically grounded and narratively tied to the specific world we are playing in.

## Ability Score Choices

You may choose one of the following methods to generate your six ability scores. 

| Generation Method | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Min-Max Array** | 18, 14, 12, 8, 8, 8 |
| **Specialist Array** | 17, 15, 13, 12, 10, 8 |
| **Generalist Array** | 15, 14, 14, 14, 12, 12 |
| **Point Buy** | 27 points to spend (Minimum score 8, Maximum score 17 before species bonuses). |
| **Old School Roll** | Roll 4d6, drop the lowest die. Do this six times. Then assign the scores to your. |

!!! info "DM's Thoughts: Rolling vs. Reliability"
    I include the 'Old School Roll' with a swap option because discovering your character's strengths through the dice, rather than engineering them, can lead to highly memorable roleplay. However, Point Buy or an Array are highly recommended if you prefer a tightly balanced build.

## Starting Gear & Money

When creating a character, you must decide how you acquired your starting equipment. Choose one of the following paths:

* **Standard Issue:** Take the starting equipment packages granted by your class and background. 
* **Starting Wealth:** Forgo the equipment packages entirely and roll for starting gold based on your class table in the *Player's Handbook* (Chapter 5). You must purchase all weapons, armor, and packs from this pool.
* **Bonus Trinket:** Regardless of your choice above, every character begins with one item rolled randomly from the Gothic Trinkets table.

This changes when you start at a level higher than 4th. 

* **5th-8th level** = 500gp plus 1d10x25gp & Normal Starting Equipment
* **9th-12th level** = 5000gp plus 1d10x250gp, two uncommon items, & Normal Starting Equipment
* **13th-16th level** =12000gp  plus 1d10x250gp, two uncommon items, one rare item, & Normal Starting Equipment
* **17th-20th level**   = 20,000gp plus 1d10×250gp, three uncommon magic items, two rare items, one very rare item, normal starting equipment


!!! info "DM's Thoughts: The Value of Scarcity"
    I lean heavily into the resource management aspect of the game. Choosing to roll for starting wealth is a gamble—you might afford premium armor, or you might start with just a dagger and a backpack. Scarcity drives early-game motivation, making your first major treasure haul feel incredible.
